Technical details

How it works

  1. 1

    Extract

    A vision model (SigLIP) compares the photo with descriptions of 13 garment types, 12 colors and 8 fabrics.

  2. 2

    Abstain

    Any attribute below its confidence threshold becomes unknown. Thresholds were tuned on human-labelled photos to be right about 90% of the time when they commit.

  3. 3

    Generate

    A language model writes 2–3 sentences from the attributes only, told never to mention or guess an unknown one.

  4. 4

    Verify

    A rule-based checker reads the text back. If it names a color, garment or fabric the photo doesn't support, the text is rebuilt so it can't.

Why it's built this way

In our study on 200 human-labelled product photos, the usual approach of sending the photo straight to a vision-language model got the garment type or color wrong in about half of descriptions.

46% → 7%wrong garment type, image-only model vs. this pipeline
58% → 0%wrong fabric claims once the system may say “unknown”
0attribute errors added by the language model: every remaining error comes from the photo reader
93%of descriptions human raters would publish as is

Study: Extract, Abstain, Generate: Decomposing the Origins of Attribute Hallucination in Grounded E-Commerce Product Descriptions, Team 10, RV University. The research used 7B models on a GPU; this site runs a smaller writer (Qwen2.5-1.5B) so it works on a CPU, and adds the verify step on top.
